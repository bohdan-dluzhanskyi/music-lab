# Scientific foundations

An identity follows from its definitions or assumptions. A physical model applies under
stated conditions. A project convention selects a useful mapping, reference, or algorithm.
An empirical hypothesis requires evidence from a protocol such as those in
[Experiments](experiments.md). Mathematical regularity alone establishes neither consonance
nor a composer's intent.

Frequencies are in hertz (Hz), physical times in seconds (s), and composition time in
quarter-note beats. Angles are in radians unless specified. Numerical implementations use
JavaScript floating point. The audio input range of 20–20,000 Hz is an application limit.
It does not guarantee audibility, accurate reproduction, or valid partials at every sample rate.

## Acoustic and pitch models

| Model and classification | Formula and variables | Conditions and musical meaning | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency and period · identity | `T = 1/f`, where `T` is seconds per cycle and `f` is cycles per second | For a periodic signal with positive finite fundamental frequency. Shorter periods correspond to higher fundamentals. Aperiodic signals need a different description. | Frequency is used throughout the [music core](../src/core/music). Period is a scientific relation documented here. |
| Sinusoidal wave · physical model | `x(t) = A sin(2πft + δ)`, with amplitude `A` in signal units, time `t` in s, frequency `f > 0` in Hz, and phase `δ` in rad | Models a single spectral component. Digital reproduction requires a representable frequency and sample rate. | Sine preview is implemented through [Tone.js](../src/engines/audio/AudioEngine.ts). The sine sequence generator uses the sinusoid to vary pitch across note indices, with period measured in events. |
| Harmonic series · identity under a periodic-signal model | `fₙ = n f₀`, `n = 1, 2, …`, `f₀ > 0` in Hz. The project uses dimensionless partial amplitudes `1/n`. | Ideal harmonic spectra have integer multiples. The amplitude law is a synthesis convention. Actual instruments can exhibit different amplitudes and inharmonicity. | [Harmonics](../src/core/music/harmonics.ts) and [additive synthesis](../src/engines/audio/constants.ts). Synth partial count is limited to 32. |
| Frequency ratios and intervals · identity plus musical interpretation | `r = f₂/f₁`, `c = 1200 log₂(r)`, `r = 2^(c/1200)`. Both frequencies are positive in Hz, ratio is unitless, `c` in cents. | Order determines the sign. An octave is ratio 2 and 1,200 cents. A ratio alone does not characterize perceived consonance. | Implemented in [frequency.ts](../src/core/music/frequency.ts). |
| Equal temperament and alternatives · tuning conventions | Equal octave divisions use `f = f₀ 2^(s/N)`, with reference `f₀` in Hz, step offset `s` and integer divisions per octave `N > 0`. Just tuning chooses ratios such as `f = f₀ p/q` with positive integers `p,q`. | Twelve equal steps yield 100 cents per step. A just fifth of 3:2 differs from a 700-cent tempered fifth. A full tuning needs reference and scale definitions. | [Generic equal-division conversions](../src/core/music/frequency.ts) exist. Main theory, names, and notation are centered on 12-EDO. A complete alternative tuning workflow is future scope. |
| Logarithmic pitch · project convention based on equal ratios | `m = 69 + 12 log₂(f/440 Hz)`, `f = 440 Hz × 2^((m−69)/12)`, `c_offset = 100(m−round(m))` | For positive `f`. `m` is a fractional MIDI coordinate in semitones. Doubling frequency adds twelve. Neither the coordinate nor a nearest note name replaces the stored frequency. | [Frequency conversion](../src/core/music/frequency.ts) and [Pitch construction](../src/core/music/notes.ts). |
| Superposition and interference · linear physical model | `y(t) = Σ xᵢ(t)` in common signal units. For two equal-amplitude sine waves of the same frequency with phase difference `Δδ`, the resultant amplitude is `2A × abs(cos(Δδ/2))`. | Requires linear summation. Same-phase components reinforce and antiphase components cancel under the stated equal-amplitude condition. Clipping and limiting violate linearity. | Additive synthesis and mixing are implemented. A controlled phase or interference measurement workflow is future scope. |

## Geometry and number sequences

| Model and classification | Formula and variables | Conditions and musical meaning | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Note clock · project convention | `α = 360° log₂(f/f₀)`; clock angle is `α mod 360°`; the unwrapped inverse is `f = f₀ 2^(α/360°)`. | Positive frequencies in Hz. One octave is one turn and a semitone is 30°. Wrapping discards octave information. | [Note clock](../src/core/geometry/noteClock.ts) and angle sequences. |
| Musical logarithmic spiral · project convention | `rₘ(θ) = r₀ 2^(θ/(2π))`, with angle `θ`, radius `r₀ > 0` in plot units, and pitch mapping `f/f₀ = rₘ/r₀`. | The radius doubles per turn. The mapping visualizes octave equivalence by angle and distinguishes registers by radius. It is a geometric encoding of pitch. | [Spiral functions](../src/core/geometry/spiral.ts). |
| Golden spiral · mathematical model | `r_g(θ) = r₀ φ^(2θ/π)` in plot units. | With `r₀ > 0`, each quarter turn scales radius by `φ`. The curve supplies a comparison geometry. Musical meaning requires an explicit mapping. | [Golden spiral](../src/core/geometry/spiral.ts). |
| Spiral intersections · derived identity under the project's geometry | Let `a = ln 2`, `b = 4 ln φ`, and let `u,v` be unwrapped musical and golden angles in turns. At equal radii, `a u = b v`. At the same ray, `u−v = k` for integer `k`. Thus `u = b k/(b−a)` and `r/r₀ = exp(abk/(b−a))`. | Requires a shared center, zero ray, starting radius, and orientation. In outward-only plots use `k ≥ 0` and retain points visible on both curves. Reading `f₀ r/r₀` as Hz is the project's sonification convention. Comparing the radii at identical unwrapped angles gives only the shared start. | [Spiral ratio](../src/core/geometry/spiral.ts) agrees with the derivation. The spiral view maps the radius ratio to frequency, as does the sequence generator. |
| Golden ratio · identity and sonification | `φ = (1 + √5)/2`, fulfilling `φ² = φ + 1`. The generator uses `fₙ = f₀ φⁿ` in Hz. | `φ` is dimensionless. As a frequency ratio it represents `1200 log₂ φ` cents. Exponents and output frequencies are bounded by numeric and playback limits. | [Constants](../src/core/mathematics/constants.ts) and [frequency generator](../src/core/composition/generators.ts). |
| Golden angle · identity and mapping | `γ = 360°/φ² = 2π/φ² rad`. With note index `n`, the unwrapped mapping uses `fₙ = f₀ 2^(nγ/360°)` when `γ` is in degrees. | `n` is a nonnegative integer. The smaller golden division of a turn is distinct from the clock angle of the pitch ratio `φ`, which is `360° log₂ φ`. Both are project inputs with different pitches. | [Constants](../src/core/mathematics/constants.ts) and [generator](../src/core/composition/generators.ts), with optional octave folding. |
| Fibonacci · recurrence and limit | `F₀=0`, `F₁=1`, `Fₙ₊₂=Fₙ₊₁+Fₙ`; `Fₙ₊₁/Fₙ → φ` for positive denominators. | The index and terms are dimensionless. The frequency generator multiplies a reference in Hz by positive terms, beginning at `F₁`. Ratios need a nonzero denominator. | [Fibonacci](../src/core/mathematics/fibonacci.ts) accepts counts 0–79 and returns terms through `F₇₈` at most. Frequency generators accept 1–64 outputs. |
| Lissajous figure · mathematical model | `x = sin(aτ + δ)`, `y = sin(bτ)`, with phase `δ` in rad, positive dimensionless frequency factors `a,b`, and sampled angular parameter `τ` in rad. | Normalized coordinates. The ideal curve closes for rational `a/b`. A finite sample window can miss closure or detail. Shapes visualize ratios and phase. | [Lissajous sampler](../src/core/geometry/lissajous.ts) accepts 2–10,000 samples over a positive number of cycles of the unit phase parameter. |
| Chord geometry · project convention | For each clock angle `θᵢ` in rad, `(xᵢ,yᵢ) = (R sin θᵢ, −R cos θᵢ)`, with `R` in plot units. | The SVG orientation puts zero at the top and advances clockwise. Octave-equivalent notes coincide. Shape comparisons discard register and do not measure voicing quality by themselves. | [Chord geometry](../src/core/geometry/chordGeometry.ts). |

## Composition and analysis models

| Model and classification | Formula and variables | Conditions and musical meaning | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Euclidean rhythm · discrete construction | `hᵢ = floor((i+1)p/s) − floor(ip/s)` for step `i=0…s−1`. The implementation left-rotates by `r mod s` using nonnegative modulo. | `s` is the step count, `p` is the pulse count, and `r` is an integer rotation. The sum telescopes to `p`. It distributes onsets across an equal-step grid, with rotation setting the starting point. | [Rhythm generator](../src/core/composition/rhythm.ts), `1 ≤ s ≤ 256`, `0 ≤ p ≤ s`. Step duration and tempo determine physical timing. |
| Logistic map · dynamical model and sonification | `xₙ₊₁ = r xₙ(1−xₙ)`; the generator emits `fₙ = f₀ 2^xₙ` in Hz before updating. | `x₀` and `r` are dimensionless, with `0 < x₀ < 1` and `0 < r ≤ 4` in code. The orbit stays within one octave in the ideal model. Behavior varies with parameters and finite-precision rounding. | [Logistic frequency generator](../src/core/composition/generators.ts). |
| Classical and mathematical hybridization · project convention | `fᵢ = 2^((1−w)log₂(cᵢ) + w log₂(mᵢ))` when frequencies are expressed numerically in Hz. Equivalently, `fᵢ = cᵢ (mᵢ/cᵢ)^w`. | Equal-length aligned sequences with positive frequencies. The unitless weight `w ∈ [0,1]` interpolates pitch in log space. It does not interpolate rhythmic or harmonic function. | [Hybrid sequence](../src/core/composition/hybrid.ts). The theory core offers the same scalar interpolation. |
| Quantization · project convention | For pitch, choose `m* = argmin_{q ∈ S} abs(q−m)` and convert back to Hz. For a time grid, `b* = max(0, round(b/g) × g)`. | `S` contains allowed MIDI pitches, `m` is fractional MIDI, and `b,g` are in quarter beats with `g > 0`. Pitch ties go to the lower candidate. Composition and theory differ in candidate range and reference policy. Quantization is an explicit operation. | [Composition pitch quantization](../src/core/composition/quantization.ts), [theory quantization](../src/core/music/quantization.ts), and [beat snapping](../src/core/composition/composition.ts). |
| Mathematical analysis of works · planned methodology | Example descriptive metrics are adjacent interval `cᵢ = 1200 log₂(fᵢ₊₁/fᵢ)` in cents and inter-onset interval `dᵢ = bᵢ₊₁−bᵢ` in quarter beats. | Define event order, voice or chord grouping, tuning, and treatment of simultaneous notes before computing metrics. A pattern claim requires a specified comparison model and allowance for multiple searches. Intent and preference require separate evidence. | The interval primitives exist. Work-level extraction, comparisons, and result reporting are planned for stage 13. |

Octave folding uses `f₀ × 2^frac(log₂(f/f₀))` to map positive frequencies into
`[f₀, 2f₀)` in ideal arithmetic. This is an optional generator transform. It is distinct
from display spelling and loses the source octave unless original data are kept.
