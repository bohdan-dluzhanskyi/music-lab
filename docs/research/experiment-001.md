# Experiment 001 · Classical vs Mathematical vs Hybrid Composition

**Status** Historical comparison with supplied single-listener ratings.

## Objective and conditions

Compare three approaches to creating music.

- **Classical** used the harmonic progression Am–F–C–G.
- **Mathematical** explored the golden ratio, golden angle, Fibonacci numbers, and spiral intersections.
- **Hybrid** combined mathematical generation with musical quantization.

## Recorded results

Each criterion was rated from 1 to 10. The total is the sum of the five scores.

| Approach | Consonance | Emotionality | Melodicity | Originality | Willingness to listen again | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Classical | 10 | 7 | 10 | 5 | 10 | 42/50 |
| Mathematical | 1 | 1 | 2 | 8 | 3 | 15/50 |
| Hybrid | 5 | 4 | 4 | 9 | 4 | 26/50 |

## Observations and interpretation

For this listener and material, Classical had the highest total. Hybrid scored highest
for originality. The preliminary hypothesis is that mathematical patterns can complement
classical music theory, while their ability to replace it remains open. See
[H-001](hypotheses.md).

## Limitations

One listener supplied subjective ratings. The comparison did not isolate the variables
well enough to attribute score differences to a single factor. A verified set of original
audio artifacts is unavailable.
