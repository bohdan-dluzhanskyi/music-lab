# Working research hypotheses

All entries remain open research hypotheses. Historical ratings can motivate follow-up
studies. They do not establish general effects.

| ID | Hypothesis | Related records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H-001 | Mathematical algorithms may increase the subjective appeal of classical musical sequences. | [001](experiment-001.md), [003](experiment-003.md), [004](experiment-004.md) |
| H-002 | The relationship between musical perception and mathematical weight may be nonmonotonic. | [003](experiment-003.md), [004](experiment-004.md) |
| H-003 | Musical quantization may materially influence the perception of mathematically generated sequences. | [004](experiment-004.md). [003](experiment-003.md) kept quantization enabled, so it gives no direct enabled-versus-disabled comparison. |
| H-004 | A classical harmonic context may improve the perception of a mathematical melody. | [005](experiment-005.md) |
| H-005 | Different mathematical generators may produce distinguishable interval distributions, rhythmic structures, and geometric characteristics. | [001](experiment-001.md) and [002](experiment-002.md) motivate this line of study. A comparison across generators remains open. |

The [experiment methodology](../experiments.md) describes how to define comparisons,
measure outcomes, and report uncertainty.
